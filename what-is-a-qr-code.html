You've seen them everywhere — on restaurant menus, product packaging, business cards, and posters. But what exactly is a QR code, and how does it work? In this guide, we'll explain everything in plain language, and show you how to create your own for free using our QR Code Generator.

What Does QR Code Stand For?

QR stands for Quick Response. It was invented in 1994 by a Japanese company called Denso Wave to track parts in car manufacturing. The idea was simple: create a barcode that could hold more information and be scanned faster than traditional barcodes.

Today, QR codes are used for everything from payments to event tickets to sharing WiFi passwords.

How Does a QR Code Work?

A QR code is a two-dimensional barcode made up of black and white squares. Those squares store data in a specific pattern. When you scan the code with your phone camera:

  1. The camera reads the pattern.
  2. Software decodes the pattern back into the original data (like a website URL or text).
  3. Your phone then takes the appropriate action — opening a website, dialing a number, or joining a WiFi network.

Unlike traditional barcodes that only store numbers, QR codes can store text, URLs, contact information, WiFi credentials, and much more.

Why Are QR Codes So Popular?

  • Instant access: No need to type long URLs. One scan and you're there.
  • Free to create: Anyone can generate a QR code without paying.
  • Works everywhere: Modern smartphones have built-in QR scanners — no app needed.
  • Versatile: They can hold links, text, contact details, payment info, and more.

Where Are QR Codes Used Today?

QR codes have become part of daily life in many industries:

  • Restaurants: Instead of printed menus, many restaurants now display a QR code that links to their digital menu.
  • Business cards: Instead of typing contact details, scan a QR code to instantly add someone to your contacts.
  • Payments: Apps like PayPal, Cash App, and many banks use QR codes for quick payments.
  • Marketing: Posters and flyers use QR codes to send people to a landing page or promo video.
  • WiFi sharing: Guests can scan a code to join your WiFi without typing the password.
  • Event tickets: Concert and flight tickets are often QR codes scanned at entry.

Are QR Codes Safe?

QR codes themselves are safe — they're just a way of storing data. But like any link, you should be careful about what you scan. A malicious QR code could take you to a phishing website. Follow these simple rules:

  • Don't scan QR codes from unknown sources.
  • Check the URL preview before tapping "open" on your phone.
  • Never enter passwords or bank details on a site you reached via an unfamiliar QR code.

For codes you created yourself, there's no risk at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do QR codes expire?

No. Static QR codes (which is what our tool creates) work forever. Some "dynamic" QR codes from paid services can be edited later, but the ones you make for free never expire.

Can I change what my QR code points to?

No. Once a static QR code is created, the destination is locked in. If you need to change it, you generate a new one.

How much data can a QR code hold?

Up to about 4,000 characters, though keeping it shorter (under 300 characters) makes the code easier to scan.

Do QR codes work without internet?

Yes — the scanning works offline. But if the QR code links to a website, you'll need internet to open it.
